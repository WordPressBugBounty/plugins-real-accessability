Free accessibility widget for WordPress with 7 ready-made profiles, contrast, text size, a reading guide and more. 46 languages, no cookies.

== Description ==

Real Accessibility adds an accessibility menu to your WordPress site (you may know it as an accessibility widget or accessibility toolbar). Visitors open it from a floating button and change the site to suit them: bigger text, higher contrast, a font that is easier to read, a reading guide, no animations and more. It is built for people with low vision, dyslexia, ADHD, epilepsy or motor impairments, and for older visitors. Whatever they choose stays in place as they move from page to page.

The plugin is free, with every feature included. You don't need an account or an API key, and there is no paid version. Choose your brand color, place the button, turn the menu on, and you're done.

= Highlights =

* Free, with all 7 profiles and all 14 adjustments included.
* Translated into 46 languages, with full right-to-left support for Hebrew, Arabic, Persian and Urdu.
* Matches your brand. The whole menu takes its colors from one brand color, and text and icons switch between black and white to stay readable.
* Small and fast: about 30 KB compressed, with no jQuery, no web fonts and no requests to other servers.
* No cookies and no tracking. Visitor choices are stored in their own browser.
* Works with any theme or page builder. The menu lives in its own Shadow DOM, so your theme's CSS can't affect it.
* Works with page caching and CDNs, because the adjustments are applied in the browser.
* A live preview in the settings shows the button and the menu as you change them.

= 7 ready-made profiles =

Each profile turns on a group of adjustments with one click.

* Seizure safe: stops animations and flashing and dims colors.
* Vision impaired: high contrast, larger text and stronger headings.
* ADHD and focus: fewer distractions, a reading guide and more space between lines.
* Dyslexia: a readable font, wider letter spacing and a reading guide.
* Motor impaired: keyboard navigation, a large cursor and larger text.
* Screen reader: keyboard navigation with links and headings highlighted.
* Seniors: large text, a large cursor and clear links.

= 14 adjustments =

Display: contrast (high, inverted or grayscale), readable font, highlighted links, highlighted headings, large cursor, hidden images, a magnifier that enlarges the text under the pointer, and text alignment (left, center or right).

Text: text size, line spacing and letter spacing, each in several steps.

Reading and navigation: a reading guide that follows the pointer, a switch that stops motion, flashing and video, and keyboard navigation with a clear focus outline.

= Make it fit your site =

* Pick any brand color, and if you like, a separate color for the button icon.
* Choose a circle, rounded or square button in the size you want.
* Put the button in any of 9 positions: the corners, the middle of each edge or the center of the screen.
* Set desktop, tablet and mobile separately (position, distance from the edge, size and the side the menu opens from), or hide the button on any of them.
* Open the menu as a side panel on the left or right, or as a sheet that slides up from the bottom, which works well on phones.
* Turn off any profile or adjustment you don't want to offer.
* Open the menu from your own link. Any link to `#real-accessibility` works, for example in your footer or main menu.
* Let visitors who don't need the menu hide the button until their next visit (optional).

= Accessibility statement =

Many accessibility laws and policies expect a published accessibility statement. Pick your statement page and the menu will show an "Accessibility statement" button that links to it.

= Missing ALT text =

If an image in a post has no ALT text but the same image has ALT text in the Media Library, the plugin adds it to the page. That helps screen reader users, and it helps image search too.

= Professional accessibility audit =

The menu helps visitors, but legal compliance depends on your site's own code and content. If you'd like an expert to check your site, the plugin's "Accessibility audit" screen lets you ask Real Media's accessibility team for a free quote.

= Please note =

An accessibility menu lets visitors adjust your site. On its own it does not make a website accessible, and it does not make it compliant with WCAG, the ADA, Section 508, the European Accessibility Act (EAA) or any other standard or law. Your site's code and content need to be accessible as well.

= Privacy =

Visitor choices are saved in the visitor's own browser (localStorage). Nothing is sent to your server or to anyone else, and no cookies are set.

== Installation ==

1. In your WordPress admin, go to Plugins > Add New, search for "Real Accessibility" and click Install Now. You can also upload the plugin folder to `/wp-content/plugins/real-accessability`.
2. Activate the plugin on the Plugins screen.
3. Open Real Accessibility in the admin menu, choose your brand color and button position, and click "Turn on the accessibility menu". The menu shows up on your site right away.

== Frequently Asked Questions ==

= Is Real Accessibility really free? =

Yes. Every profile, adjustment, language and design setting is free. There's no paid version, no account and no usage limit.

= Will this plugin make my website ADA or WCAG compliant? =

No plugin can do that by itself. The menu lets visitors change how your site looks and behaves, which helps a lot of people, but WCAG, the ADA and similar laws are about the site itself: its code, content, images, forms and documents. If you want to know where your site stands, you can ask for a free quote for a professional audit on the plugin's "Accessibility audit" screen.

= Does it help with the European Accessibility Act (EAA)? =

It gives visitors useful tools and a link to your accessibility statement. Like other accessibility laws, though, the EAA applies to the website itself. Use the menu together with an accessible theme, accessible content and, ideally, an accessibility audit.

= Will the accessibility widget slow down my website? =

No. The menu's code is about 30 KB compressed. It doesn't use jQuery or web fonts, and it doesn't load anything from other servers.

= Does it use cookies? Is it GDPR friendly? =

It sets no cookies and sends nothing about your visitors anywhere. Each visitor's choices are saved in their own browser (localStorage), so they carry over from page to page.

= Will it work with my theme or page builder? =

Yes. The plugin adds the menu itself and runs it in an isolated Shadow DOM, so it works with any theme or page builder, and your theme's styles can't change how it looks.

= Does it work with caching plugins and CDNs? =

Yes. All adjustments are applied in the visitor's browser, so cached pages work as usual.

= Can I change the color, position and shape of the button? =

Yes. You can set the brand color, the icon color, a circle, rounded or square shape, the size, the corner radius, one of 9 positions and the distance from the edge of the screen.

= Can I use different settings on mobile, or hide the button there? =

Yes. Desktop, tablet and mobile each have their own position, size, distance from the edge and menu side, and you can hide the button on any of them.

= Can I open the menu from my own link? =

Yes. Any link to `#real-accessibility` opens the menu, for example a link in your footer, your main menu or your accessibility statement page.

= Which languages are supported? =

46 languages and regional variants, including English, Spanish, Portuguese, Arabic, Russian, Hebrew, French, German, Italian, Dutch, Polish, Turkish, Ukrainian, Japanese, Chinese, Korean and Hindi. You decide which ones the menu offers, and visitors switch between them from the top of the menu.

= Does it support right-to-left languages? =

Yes. Hebrew, Arabic, Persian and Urdu are fully supported. The menu and the settings screens both switch to right-to-left.

= How do I add a language that isn't listed? =

Add a complete translation of Real Accessibility to your site, either from translate.wordpress.org or as a .mo file in `wp-content/languages/plugins`. It will then show up under Real Accessibility > Languages.

= Does the plugin write my accessibility statement? =

No. You publish the statement as a normal page on your site, and the plugin adds an "Accessibility statement" button to the menu that links to it.

= Can I turn off features I don't need? =

Yes. You can turn off any profile or adjustment on the Features screen, and the menu will only show the ones you keep.

= Can you check my website for accessibility issues? =

Yes. Go to Real Accessibility > Accessibility audit in your admin and fill in the short form to get a free quote for a professional audit.

= Does the plugin add a link or credit to my site? =

Only if you say yes. When you turn the menu on, you can choose to show a small credit at the bottom of the menu with a link to the plugin page on WordPress.org, or turn it on without one. You can change this at any time under Real Accessibility > Features.

= Why is there a Cloudways banner in the settings? =

The Button and menu, Features and Languages screens show a Cloudways hosting banner (or a WooCommerce hosting banner if WooCommerce is active). It's an affiliate link, so if you sign up through it, REALMEDIA may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. While one of those screens is open, the banner image loads from cloudways.com, which means Cloudways receives your browser's IP address and user agent. There is no impression counter or tracking script, and nothing from Cloudways is loaded on your site's front end or shown to your visitors.

= What happened to the page reader from version 1? =

It was removed in version 2.0. Screen readers, and the reading features that come with browsers and operating systems, do this job better.
